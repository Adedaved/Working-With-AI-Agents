# Reasoning

[Chapter overview](README.md) | [Course home](../README.md)

## In plain English

Reasoning links available evidence to decisions about what to do next. In a workflow it might decide whether a source is relevant, whether a result meets a rule, or whether another tool call is needed. Keep decisions testable: cite the rule and the observed facts. Request concise decision summaries and action logs rather than private internal deliberation. Verification should use outputs and evidence that a reviewer can inspect.

## Try it: original exercise

1. State a decision rule: classify only from the documented ticket description.
2. Record the evidence supporting each classification.
3. Flag ambiguous cases and explain the specific missing fact.
4. Compare decisions with a human-reviewed answer set.

## Check your understanding

What makes a decision explanation useful to a reviewer?

**Answer checkpoint:** The applicable rule, observable evidence and a clear account of uncertainty.

## Common mistake

A fluent explanation does not prove the reasoning was correct or that a claimed tool action occurred.
